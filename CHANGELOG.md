# Changelog

## 0.4.0 — 2026-10-31

- `scorify/theory`: spelled scales (`SCALES`, `TONICS`, `scaleById`, `scalePitches`): major, natural, harmonic and melodic minor, major and minor pentatonic, blues, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian and chromatic.
- `scorify/songs`: practice pieces. `scalePiece`, `scaleScore`, `scaleOnNeck`, `scaleRhythms` and `SCALE_BPM` write a scale exercise for one instrument (the lowest octave the neck holds, never moved or simplified: `ScorePiece.exercise`). `PROGRESSIONS` ranks the most common chord progressions of the McGill Billboard corpus (Hot 100, 1958–1991; counted by scorify), and `progressionSong` with `PROGRESSION_VERSIONS` plays them in 4/4, 3/4, 6/8 and 12/8, seven versions each; `chordOfNumeral` reads roman numerals.
- Docs playground: the library is in vertical tabs, **Songs**, **Scales** and **Chord progressions**; the Key select sets a scale's tonic and moves a progression.
- Player profiles: `highFretCosts`, a soft cost per fret above a threshold for the highest fretted note (counted from the capo), per quarter note: baby to semi-pro 1 per fret above 8 and 4 more per fret above 10, pro and expert 4 per fret above 10, maestro none. It ranks after musical loss, so lower difficulties play lower on the neck without losing notes; an attempt whose search runs out of budget with it is searched once more without it (log entries marked `anyPosition`, reported in the notes). Search results change for every level but maestro.
- `concertUkulele()` defaults to 18 frets (was 19). `scorify/songs`: the baritone ukulele has 18 frets and the mandolin 20; `highestFret(id)`; a request's `frets` sets another highest fret (`customInstrumentId(base, { frets })`, id `~frets12`), a hard limit reported in the arrangement notes; the usual one keeps the cache key. `suggestCapo` keeps five frets above a capo.
- Docs playground: a **Highest fret** slider next to Tempo, the instrument's usual highest fret marked, remembered per instrument. Custom mode's player limit is now labelled **Player's highest fret**.
- `scorify/songs`: progression styles. `progressionSong(progression, time, { style })` and `scaleChordsSong` play chords strummed (`strum`) or picked as an arpeggio (`arpeggio-up`, `arpeggio-down`, `arpeggio-up-down`; `PROGRESSION_STYLES`, `arpeggioVersions`, `ARPEGGIO_TIMES`, a version's `arpeggio` layer): single tones walking the strings of each chord's voicing, from two notes a bar at baby to sixteenths at maestro. The strum versions (`PROGRESSION_VERSIONS`) now strum every hit at every difficulty, and none strikes more than three quarters of the places on its grid: the hardest syncopate instead of filling the bar. Before, from pro on (and amateur in 3/4) they picked arpeggios over an alternating bass. Arpeggio versions start their search with a beam of 512 (`ARPEGGIO_SEARCH`; a narrow beam fingered an inverted ukulele F across eight frets), and retries only use wider tiers. Times on thirds of a quarter are exact (24ths) instead of rounded to 32nds.
- `scorify/songs`: strumming patterns. `STRUM_PATTERNS` (sixteen, by style: island, reggae and ska; rock, pop and funk; folk, boom-chick and bluegrass chop; swing and blues shuffle; waltz; 6/8 ballad), `strumPattern(id)` and `progressionSong(…, { pattern })`, which plays one as a progression's version at its tempo. Each is written as players write it (`writtenStrokes`), with the instruments it is typical on and its measured difficulty; bass patterns need six strings. A hit's `length` makes a short strum.
- `scorify/theory`: `scalePitches` plays a scale `down` from the top or `down-up` as well (`ScaleDirection`); a `ScaleExercise` has a `direction`. `scorify/songs`: `scaleTriads` (the triad on every degree of a seven-note scale) and `scaleChordsSong`, the scale's chords up to the octave and back.
- `scorify/songs`: chord inversions. A request's `inversions`, keyed by chord segment (`segmentKey`, the chord as played), name the chord tone to put in the bass; the chord is voiced again with it lowest (`voiceChord(…, { inversion })`, `chordShapes(…, { inBass })`, `Voicing.inversion`), an alternating bass follows it, and the song is searched again under the difficulty's limits. `prepare` returns the chords as played (`chords`: `ChordSegment`), reports the inverted ones and keys that name no chord; `chordInversion` and `INVERSION_NAMES` name inversions by the lowest sounding pitch. The cache key includes inversions when there are any.
- Fingering model: a held finger keeps its fret but may widen its contact there, laying a barre flat around a note it holds; before, the footprint was fixed while any of its notes sounded, and picked arpeggios of barre chords above amateur were reported infeasible on guitar. Search results change; the public cache is recomputed (cache version `8ac1bc6e57f9755c`).
- Docs playground: on Scales and Chord progressions, **Time** and **Play** selects follow Key (scale up and down, up, down, down and up, or its chords strummed or arpeggiated; a progression strummed or arpeggiated), and a strummed progression has **Pattern**: its versions by difficulty or a strumming pattern for the meter, grouped by style. Every chord name in the tab of a chord song has two extra-small buttons: the next inversion, and back as written; an inversion searches again, shows its bass (F/A), and when it does not play the page offers **Play the chords as written**. The library is remembered under `scorify-demo-library-2`.
- Docs playground: the page opens at maestro again (20% above the piece's tempo).
- Docs playground: the metronome switch is called **Beat** and is on by default, as are Tape delay, Reverb and Saturation. The switches are remembered under new keys (`scorify-demo-practice-2`, `scorify-demo-effects-2`), so choices saved earlier do not hide the new defaults.
- Docs site and README: install from npm (bun, npm, pnpm, yarn) or, without a bundler, from jsDelivr with an import map (a Worker or AudioWorklet still comes from the page's own origin, via `workletUrl`). The repository is a Claude Code plugin marketplace (`.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`): `/plugin install score-import --marketplace kyr0/scorify` installs the skill; Codex installs it with `$skill-installer` from `skills/score-import`, where the skill now lives (moved from `skill/`). The footer links to the rendered docs on GitHub, the npm package and the issue tracker.
- Docs site: the ukulele example strums its C chord (`rightHand: "strum"`); picked, its four notes exceed beginner's three picked notes per bar, so it returned `infeasible` in 0.3.0.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-10-30

- Add a docs site (`docs/index.html`) built from defuss-shadcn v0.9.11: landing page, API overview and installation guide. Its playground arranges three hand-authored accompaniment studies (ukulele island strum, guitar bass and strum, guitar 3/4 arpeggio) in a module worker, draws the solver's fingerings with the Fretboard, Neck and Chord Chart components, and plays the validated performance.
- Add `bun run check:docs`, a Chromium check of the docs site.
- Docs playground: re-arrange every piece for eight instruments (concert ukulele high/low G, baritone ukulele, guitar standard and drop D, 7- and 8-string guitar, mandolin). Chord shapes are found per instrument and level and probed with `arrange`. Open `.scorify.json` files in the browser; a public-domain Greensleeves MIDI is bundled as an imported example, with a voices control and stated articulation and range changes.
- Add the `score-import` skill (`skill/`): a standalone Python tool that visits pages with CloakBrowser (honoring robots.txt, stopping at challenge/login/paywall walls), downloads score files with a provenance sidecar, converts MusicXML/MXL, ABC and MIDI to scorify Score JSON (exact time, spelled sounding pitch, voices, roles, ties, repeats, pickups, chord symbols, source) and validates the result. Scores are for private use; the skill does not track licenses.
- Docs playground: every song has seven hand-authored versions, one per difficulty (baby to maestro), from a single strum or bass note per bar to fills and alternating bass; the selected difficulty plays the hardest version written for it or below that fits the instrument, and simplifies the easiest one with adaptive mode only when none does. "Player policy" is now "Difficulty"; the Auto level is gone. House of the Rising Sun shows its traditional tune's words under the tab. Play, Pause and Resume share one button; the sound is the offline-rendered performance, so Space and a looped selection play audio and the playhead follows the audio clock. Opened scores are remembered in the browser. Wider search budgets: beam 32 with 512 candidates per frame, retried with 128/2,048 and then 2,048/16,384 when exhausted.
- score-import skill: keep lyrics, every verse (ABC `w:`/`W:`, MusicXML `<lyric>`), and mark sung parts in `notation.vocal`; the playground shows them as lanes of words and does not play them. Add score sets (`scorify-set-1`, `*.scorify-set.json`) and `score.py bundle`, which packs several versions of one piece with a given or estimated difficulty; `check` validates sets. Licenses are no longer tracked (private use).
- Add live pitch detection and tuning. `scorify/pitch-detect`: YIN (`detectPitch`) and a streaming tracker with median smoothing (`createPitchTracker`), host-neutral and allocation-free per window. `scorify/pitch-worklet`: an AudioWorklet processor that runs the tracker on live input. `scorify/tuner`: `createPitchInput` (microphone without voice processing, or any stream or node, into the worklet; `configure`, idempotent `stop` that releases only a microphone it opened), `tuningTargets` from a fretted instrument (capo, concert A), `nearestTarget`, `centsFrom`, `chromaticNote`. The root module graph stays free of them; the pitch worklet graph loads no score or solver code.
- Docs playground: a tuner above the player, the defuss-shadcn tacho showing the string of the chosen instrument nearest to what the microphone hears (or a picked string), its cents and whether it is in tune, and a reference pluck of the string.
- Performance: strums are centered on their beat by default (`anchor`, 0.5) and, without `spreadSeconds`, sweep at a tempo-scaled speed (`strumSweepSeconds`: a twentieth of a quarter across all strings, 12–60 ms; up-strokes quicker, partial strokes shorter). Humanization adds played dynamics (`metricWeight` bar accents, melody and bass accents, a strum's `taper` from its first string, up-strokes lighter, harder plucks brighter) and a slow per-string drift (`detuneCents`, within ±`driftCents`, always under one cent; the written `frequency` stays exact). The DSP plays `detuneCents`; validation bounds it and checks anchored strum offsets.
- Docs playground: the feel is a fixed loose human one (`humanize` 0.8); the Feel control is gone. The tempo slider marks the original tempo, and the mark restores it. The active chord's chart leads the voicings in a golden aura, the next chord stands beside it and blinks with the beat in the bar before it comes; charts show one hand position, so no dot falls outside a chart. The neck scrolls to keep the sounding notes in view. The end of a song no longer flickers between the end and the start. Every playback and every loop pass is a new take with a new seed, rendered in queued segments with short crossfades. The tab's bar numbers are a loop header: drag forward to loop several bars, double-click or drag back to clear the loop.
- Worker endpoint: yield and post progress per ~12 ms time slice instead of every 32 steps. Browsers clamp nested `setTimeout(0)` to ≥ 4 ms, which made long searches several times slower.
- Docs playground: chord charts for imported scores (`scorify-chart-1`, `songFromChart`). A local practice entry may name a `*.chart.json`; its card then plays seven strummed versions written for the edition's bars under the score's sung part, words and tempo map, instead of adapting the piano part. Song bars may hold several chords (`"C Am"`), `N.C.` and held bars; versions marked `simple` play a chart's simple chords; strum-only versions may set `countStrokes`, an explicit, reported attack-rate override that counts strokes instead of strings, so beginners strum full chords; a chart may set its default `bpm`; `vocalTranspose` moves (and reports) a sung passage written in another key. The chord finder ranks shapes that stay within the fourth fret before shapes with more strings (guitar D `xx0232`, A `x02220`, A7 `x02020`; ukulele D7 `2223`), and the chosen shapes become scorify `familiarShapes` (except for versions with an alternating bass), so a strum is not fingered high up the neck. A held final chord's strum is placed where it is struck (Wicked Game's maestro ending was plucked without a strum).
- Docs playground: precomputed arrangements for every piece, instrument and difficulty at its own tempo (`bun run precompute:docs`, checked by `bun run check:cache`; `--private` for the local library), replayed only when their inputs and the scorify build fingerprint alike and after `validateFrettedArrangement` accepts them; searched results are kept in IndexedDB. A suggested capo (`suggestCapo`: fewer non-open chord shapes at no higher cost) appears as a toggle, e.g. "Capo on fret 2 · Am G D shapes"; its other side is precomputed or searched in a second worker, so it switches at once, and chord names show the fingered shapes. A collapsible Custom mode sets the version, mode, transposition, capo, tuning, player limits, adaptation permissions, search settings and playback feel, re-computes, and exports (`scorify-arrangement-1`), saves in the browser and imports arrangements.
- Docs playground: opens at amateur; maestro starts every piece 20% above its tempo (`LEVEL_TEMPO`, `tempoFor`). A tempo change replays a cached arrangement, or keeps the fingering on show when scorify's validator and the performance check accept it at the new tempo and no harder version could fit a slower one (`retime`, reported with the tempo it was searched at), and searches otherwise. `precompute:docs` keeps fresh entries and searches only the stale ones. The notes fact ("Notes vs. the maestro version") compares the notes played with the hardest version as written on the same instrument (`hardestNotes`), with a percentage.
- Docs playground: no source sites on the page (the bundled Greensleeves keeps only its checksum and retrieval date; imported scores show no source link or site credit). The line under the transport shows only progress and problems; the explanation of each arrangement (version, fallbacks, overrides, notes kept, cache or retime) moved to the collapsed **About this arrangement**, and the playback clock is gone. The playground intro no longer explains the tuner and loops.
- Add `scorify/songs`: the arrangement layer the playground runs on, now part of the library. Chord-chart songs with a version per difficulty (`down`, `up`, `pick`, alternating bass, fills, holds), the chord finder (`voiceChord`, `chordShapes`), `sourceFor`, imported pieces (`pieceFromScore`, score sets, `estimateLevel`), chord charts (`songFromChart`), the request flow with logged fallbacks (`arrangeFor`, `prepare`, `versionsFor`, `limitsExceeded`), cached replay with validation (`fingerprint`, `cacheKey`, `attemptFingerprint`, `cacheable`), `retime`, `suggestCapo`, `strokesFor`, `voicingsFor`, `hardestNotes`, `LEVEL_TEMPO`/`tempoFor` and the `INSTRUMENTS` catalog with capo and tuning ids.
- Add post effects to the audio library (`scorify/audio-effects`, also in `audio-dsp` and `audio`): soft saturation, tape delay and reverb in one deterministic, block-size-independent chain (`EffectChain`, `applyEffects`, `effectsTailSeconds`, `delayForTempo`). `renderPerformance(…, { effects })`, `player.play(plan, { effects })`, `player.setEffects()`, and `createEffectsNode()` for any Web Audio source (processor `scorify-effects-v1` in `audio-worklet.mjs`). Worklet and offline samples are identical.
- `validateFrettedArrangement` sweeps active notes and the attack window forward over ordered frames instead of filtering all notes per frame (identical diagnostics; about 10× faster on long songs). Docs playground: a result saved in this browser by an earlier build no longer hides the precomputed cache (layers are merged, IndexedDB entries carry their build); Greensleeves' voice choices are precomputed (392 public configurations).
- score-import skill: a tablature staff that repeats the notation staff of its part (staff and tab shown together) is dropped and counted instead of doubling every note; in a score of tablature only, an unseen key signature without sharps or flats is not taken as C major (the key is unknown). The local practice library accepts score sets in `precompute-docs.mjs --private` and `check-private-docs.mjs`, which checks every difficulty of a set.
- Player profiles: picked notes per bar (`maxPickedPerBar`: baby 2, beginner 3, amateur 4, semi-pro 6, pro 8, none above) when the right hand picks, unless each note sounds alone, so the limit thins notes picked together, never the tune's moments; strums do not count. The search, the validator and `limitsExceeded` apply it, results report `maxPickedInOneBar`, and `barAt` gives a time's bar.
- Adaptive search keeps a song's identity: leaving a moment of the source without any of its non-ornament notes costs 24 in musical loss, doubled notes that would starve the bar's later moments are thinned first, the beam ranks paths with the loss the bar must still incur, and notes that must still start in a bar are reserved. In a melody chord of an imported score only the top note is the melody.
- Search paths share their history (persistent lists) instead of copying it per candidate: identical results, memory per path independent of the piece's length, and long searches several times faster (Wicked Game, 1,997 notes: 50 s → 6 s for the first tier). `precompute-docs.mjs` replaces a worker that fails and reports its configuration, and reuses the files of a run that stopped before writing its index.
- Add practice sounds (`scorify/audio-guide`, also in `audio`): `renderGuideVoice` sings a line as a soft synthesized voice (four partials, delayed vibrato, a 1.8 kHz two-pole lowpass), `renderClicks` plays a metronome of a sine kick (120 → 45 Hz) and a noise clap (three bursts through a 1.8 kHz bandpass), each through a −24 dB, 4:1 compressor, after defuss-shadcn's envelope demo; `clickPattern` and `countIn` give a meter's clicks. Spans render alike in segments.
- Docs playground: a **Voice** switch sings the sung part (on by default; disabled without one), a **Metronome** switch adds the kick and clap (off by default), both through gains that take effect at once; the voice goes through the effects, the metronome stays dry, and the WAV includes what is switched on. Playback from a stop or pause counts in one bar (clicks, the playhead held at the start; a pickup falls into its last beats); a loop's next pass does not. A **No loop / Loop** swap (defuss-shadcn swap) plays the song again from the beginning when it ends. The playback clock follows each rendered segment, so loops of bars and the song loop are rendered in pieces too.
- `createPerformance(…, { timeLimits: false })` plays a fingering validated at another tempo: only the time-based player limits (attacks per second, shift and finger speed, finger setup) are lifted, recorded in the plan (`timeLimits: "lifted"`) and its diagnostics; `validatePerformance` follows the record. The docs playground performs every take this way, so a tempo change no longer ends in "This fingering cannot perform that timing".
- An imported score's amateur chord version lets go a sixteenth before each strum (`release`), like the easier ones: held to the next chord it left the hand no time to move, and Bamboléo found no amateur arrangement in any key.
- The chord finder's shape probe ignores the picked-notes limit, as it ignores the attack rate: it tests the fretting hand. Since the limit, a version that picks only the root (Wicked Game's and Rising Sun's baby versions) found no three-note shape at baby and was simplified.
- Docs playground: the arrangement cache is named by `src/VERSION` instead of a hash of the built library, so library changes that do not change arrangements keep it; change VERSION to search every cached configuration again.
- Docs playground: every card and the rhythm badge call a piece a "Song" (before: Ukulele, Guitar, Imported, Private song or Private score).
- Docs playground: changing the tempo keeps the place: playback goes on from the same beat without a count-in, a pause stays where it was, and a loop of bars is kept (before, it stopped and rewound to the start).
- `check:packed` fails if the npm package contains anything but `dist/`, `package.json`, `README.md`, `LICENSE` and `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt`: the docs site, its caches, `work/`, the skill, sources and tests stay out.
- `scorify/songs`: the tempo is playback only. Requests are searched at the piece's own tempo and `retime` scales any result to another tempo without a search; cache keys omit the tempo. A request's `transpose` (the key selector; custom transposition wins) is part of the cache key; `KEY_SHIFTS` and `keyName` name the keys. Imported scores get strummed chords inferred from their notes (`ScorePiece.chords`), played first at baby and beginner and last above; `versionById` finds them. Chord charts take `sungLine` (the part carrying the tune per span of bars) and `lyricLanguages`.
- Docs playground: a Key select next to Difficulty, every key precomputed; the round Play Button; tempo changes never search.
- score-import skill: parts named for a voice in German, French, Spanish, Italian or Russian (`Gesang`, `Voix`, `Голос`, …) are sung parts.
- Docs playground: Tape delay, Reverb (default on) and Saturation switches beside the transport, using the library effects live and in the WAV; a Print button with title, tab at page width and static chord charts; a section on installing the score-import skill in Claude Code and Codex.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-10-28 (unpublished)

- Add optional performance, audio DSP, browser-player and AudioWorklet entry points; core imports stay free of audio code.
- Add persistent per-string Karplus–Strong waveguides, fractional-delay tuning, pick-position/brightness excitation, rapid muting, body resonators, stereo pan and optional saturation.
- Add physical-order up/down/partial/muted strums and explicit rational-time patterns.
- Add deterministic humanization by seed and event/stroke identity: bounded timing, velocity, brightness, position and decay; fresh excitation on every attack.
- Replay actual attack/release timings against fingering constraints. Preserve every sounding duration and tied identity; reduce timing jitter explicitly or return a selected-fingering failure.
- Add prepared sample-frame playback, stale-request rejection, stop/fade/dispose, offline Float32 rendering and PCM16 WAV export.
- Extend the browser demo, packed declaration consumer, adversarial tests and actual Chromium/worklet checks. Include four original WAV studies, pitch/CPU benchmarks and the complete audio contract.
- Document approximate timbres, model limits, device-validation gaps and future streaming/technique work.
